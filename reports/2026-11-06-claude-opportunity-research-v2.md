The core finding is that **2026 Etsy is flooded with new AI-made spreadsheet listings that have zero sales.** I saw this in every niche I checked (Section 3). The "AI builds templates" plan is now what everyone does, so it only works where the product shows real knowledge of the trade and the listing earns its first reviews. That points to niches where Cash or an AI can show real domain depth, and to a test that measures demand rather than predicting it.

**What this shows:** buyers pay in established niches like landlords [V]. Hobby and trade niches show small but real purchases [V]. One seller report ([Side Hustle Nation](https://www.sidehustlenation.com/selling-spreadsheets-on-etsy/)) says a $250k shop won on design and a defined buyer, and that ads paid off only on $20 to $30 items [S].

**Pottery angle:** Cash runs a pottery account (README), so Cash can judge whether a potter's template is actually useful. That is a real quality edge [A]. Against it: the only pottery listing with reviews sells for $1.26 [V], and potters have free substitutes such as the open Glazy recipe library ([derekau.net](https://derekau.net/blog/glazy-open-source-ceramics-recipes-library/)) and free kiln-log guidance from [Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Advice-for-Keeping-A-Kiln-Log). The business side (pricing, cost of goods, craft-fair inventory) is where a paid workbook beats free tools [A].

### Competition
Established: landlord, budget and planners. Thin but contested by new zero-sale shops: livestock, pottery, groomers, craft fairs [V]. Search-engine discovery undercounts listings, so competition is probably higher than it looks here [A].

### First-customer strategy
1. Etsy search on long-tail trade terms (free).
2. Cash's pottery followers, for the pottery listings only. Disclose that Cash is the seller.
3. Optional Etsy Ads later, only on items priced $20 or more [S, same seller].

Offsite Ads charge 15% on attributed sales; shops under $10k a year can opt out ([Etsy fees](https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/)) [V].

### Startup and ongoing costs [V unless marked]
- $0.20 per listing, renewed every 4 months or after each sale.
- 6.5% transaction fee.
- US payment processing of 3% + $0.25 ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015628847-What-are-Payment-Processing-Fees-for-Selling-on-Etsy), confirmed by Instinct).
- A one-time shop setup fee "may be required"; Etsy's fee page states no amount.
- No tools are needed beyond Google Sheets [A].

### Unit economics
On a $15 sale: listing $0.20, transaction $0.98, processing about $0.70. That leaves about $13.10 **contribution after listed platform fees, not net profit** [A arithmetic on the fees above], or about $10.85 if Offsite Ads attribute the sale. It excludes ads, taxes, refunds and any setup fee. Marginal cost per extra copy is $0. No sales volume is assumed or predicted.

### Etsy's Oct 22, 2026 AI policy: unresolved compliance risk
Etsy's [Seller Handbook article of Oct 22, 2026](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1522733284810) says [V]:
- Listings bot-generated without any human involvement aren't allowed, and the shop can be removed.
- AI-generated functional guides, such as patterns, recipes and instructions, are banned. The information buyers rely on must be the seller's own work.
- AI use must still be disclosed, and AI work can't be passed off as entirely the seller's own creation.

A spreadsheet template and its how-to guide are functional content buyers rely on. A fully AI-built, hands-off product line is therefore at real risk under this policy [A]. An owner's okay can't waive it. Mitigations, still to be confirmed against Etsy's own wording [A]:
- Cash makes the design decisions.
- Cash checks every formula against real use.
- Cash writes or substantively rewrites the instructions.
- Listing images are real screenshots, not AI imagery.

This reduces how hands-off the plan can be, and is the biggest open question for rank 1.

### Biggest failure risks
- Zero sales because the niche has no searches.
- Copycats undercutting price within weeks.
- Etsy account risk with one platform.
- Price compression to $5, as in the landlord niche.

- **Not proven by a pass:** market size. A pass only shows that this listing can sell.

## 4. Rank 3: TikTok Shop affiliate on the pottery account

**Product:** commissions on pottery tools and supplies that Cash already uses, sold through TikTok Shop showcase links.

**Evidence:**
- Cash's account (~1,300 followers per the README) clears the 1,000-follower minimum. Under 5,000 starts a limited 30-day pilot with no campaign access ([Creators Agency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/tiktok-shop-affiliate-requirements-2026), citing TikTok Shop Academy) [V on page, secondary].
- Templates themselves can't be sold in TikTok Shop: digital goods are invite-only ([EchoTik](https://www.echotik.live/blog/can-you-sell-digital-products-on-tiktok-shop/)) [V on page, secondary].
- Commission rates are seller-set, often 5% to 25% ([OneCart](https://getonecart.com/tiktok-shop-seller-fees/)) [V on page, secondary].

**Workload:** Cash films hands-on pottery content, which is the main cost. AI can script videos, pick products and track results.

**Risk:** small, irregular commissions [A]. The upside is that it builds the audience for the pottery templates [A].

**Test [T, needs approval]:** apply, then post 10 product-featuring videos over 30 days. Measure showcase clicks and commissions.

## 6. Rank 5: paid craft/art-fair data (checked because Cash is a maker)

**Paid demand exists [V]:**
- FestivalNet (copyright notice from 1996) sells a Pro tier with full contacts, ratings and application tracking ([FAQ](https://festivalnet.com/faq?tag=price)).
- Art Fair SourceBook sells artist subscriptions with ratings and sales results for 700+ fairs, and sells organizers email promos to 4,000+ artists ([AFSB](https://artfairsourcebook.com/)).

**Why not now:** basic calendars are free (for example [The Craft Map](https://thecraftmap.com/)) [V]. What artists pay for is ratings and sales-result data, which a new entrant doesn't have and has to collect from exhibitors [A].

## 7. Rank 6: entry-level remote job board, re-challenged with the README's own case

- **The cited success case [S]:** the [5k MRR board](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/my-job-board-has-passed-5k-mrr-after-3-years-of-building-527416c1c1) took about 3 years and 653 commits, with a scraper run by hand every two days for almost two years. It earned $10 in its first month of AdSense. Its first paid listing came in March 2025. That is a full-time-scale effort, not minimal work.
- **Feeds [V]:** aggregator backfill pays roughly $0.05 to $0.25 a click. Indeed's publisher program has been paused since Oct 2022, and ZipRecruiter ended ZipSearch on Mar 31, 2025 ([Cavuno](https://cavuno.com/blog/job-feeds)).
- **Duplicate listings:** licensed feeds carry the same listings as everyone else, so they rank poorly (same source).
- **Saturation:** "remote" is named as a saturated niche, with WeWorkRemotely at about 4.5M visitors ([Niceboard](https://niceboard.co/learn/building/5-mistakes-why-job-boards-fail-solutions)) [S, vendor]. Entry-level remote is the most contested part of that market [A].
- **Failure cases:** a data-jobs board closed after 15 months with no revenue ([ProjectBI](https://projectbi.net/blog/why-im-shutting-down-data-jobs-board)) [S]. A Gulf board got about 15 paying employers in 2 years and only earned steadily once it charged job seekers ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/2-years-of-running-a-niche-job-board-what-id-do-differently-in-2026)) [S].
- **Not verified by me:** the claim that Pieter Levels abandoned an AI job board for lack of demand.
- **Cheapest kill test [T, free]:** for 20 target queries, record whether Google shows its jobs panel and which boards rank. Also confirm in writing that each of the three licensed feeds allows republishing and pays. If the panel dominates or any feed doesn't pay, stop.

## 10. Test against Cash's target: $5-10k/month, low work, low entry cost

- **Niche templates alone are unlikely to reach the target** [A]. They are a cheap first step that tests whether the team can make things that sell.
