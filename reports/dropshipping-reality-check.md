## 1. What sellers report now [S, Shopify Community]
- "Is dropshipping still profitable in and after 2025": replies from Oct 2025 to **Jun 2026** say it's "still profitable but harder", with tighter margins and tougher competition. What works, they say, is a niche, branding, **fast (US) shipping** and customer experience. Zendrop and CJ Dropshipping are named. One reply carries an **affiliate-tagged Shopify link**. **No reply gives margin, ad-cost or refund figures.** https://community.shopify.com/t/is-dropshipping-still-profitable-in-and-after-2025-for-new-shopify-stores/569817
- **Payout holds** are a recurring failure mode:
  - **Apr 9 2026:** a seller reports a **30% hold for 120 days** after a supplier fulfilment delay and a rise in chargebacks, on a 15% margin. https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-hold-for-120-days-and-30-hold/607089
  - **Aug 10 2024:** a **20% reserve for 120 days**, with Shopify's Merchant Trust citing dropshipping fulfilment-time risk, even though the seller says she had no chargebacks. https://community.shopify.com/t/20-reserve-on-payouts-because-of-dropshipping/347340/1
  - Neither thread shows a staff reply.

## 3. Sourcing rules that change the picture [V owning source]
- **eBay** allows dropshipping "from a wholesale supplier", but "purchasing the item from another retailer or marketplace that ships directly to your customer is not allowed". The seller stays "responsible for the safe delivery… within the time frame stated in your listing". https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/selling-policies/drop-shipping-policy?id=4176
- **Retail arbitrage** (buying from Amazon or Walmart and shipping to the buyer) is therefore **out on eBay**. Amazon's and Walmart's own policies weren't read here [T].
- **Etsy:** under its Creativity Standards (read earlier on this thread), physical items must be made, designed or handpicked by the seller. [A] Generic dropshipped goods don't fit Etsy.

## 4. Profitability reality check [A unless marked]
- **Credit card funding:** the average rate on US credit card **accounts assessed interest was 22.36% (Aug 2026)** [V, Federal Reserve G.19, released Oct 7 2026]. That's a national average, **not Cash's rate**. At that level, a balance carried a month costs about **1.9%**.
- **Cash flow order:** ads and supplier cost go out on the card *first*. Payouts arrive later and can be **held 20–30% for 120 days** [S above]. So working capital needs are higher than per-sale margin suggests, and card interest compounds on what's held.
- **Per-sale margin:** sellers in the dated threads report about **15%** (one self-report) [S]. With an ad cost per sale unknown [T], refunds, and chargebacks costing order value plus fees, a 15% margin can go negative on one bad week.
- **Workload:** product testing, ad management, customer service for slow shipments, refunds and disputes, all daily. **It fails the hands-off rule**, the same as the FB Marketplace finding.
- **Survivorship bias:** forum and Reddit success posts come from people still in business. Failed stores rarely post, and many "how I made $X" posts sell courses or apps.

## Reading [A], preliminary
- It's **still possible**, but the evidence I can reach shows it's harder than four years ago: tighter margins, US-shipping expectations, payout reserves, and the platform rules above.
- Funding it by credit card at around 22% adds a fixed drag *before* any product is proven.
- Against Cash's hands-off rule it's the highest-labor option on this thread.
- **I wouldn't recommend card-funded dropshipping as the next step.** If he wants to test it, the low-risk version is a small, capped budget on a POD or US-warehouse product with a stop rule. That would be his decision and a separate spend approval.
