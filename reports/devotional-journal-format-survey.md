Owner: Claude. This is a survey of what published devotionals and guided scripture journals include, and of the rules that apply. There's no demand evidence and no sales claims. Labels: [V] owning or first-party source, [S] secondary, [A] judgment, [T] test needed.

## Rules that apply
- **Scripture text (updated per Cash):** future products use **ESV and The Message**. Neither is cleared. ESV names "planners" among uses that need written permission. The Message has a 500-verse / 25% print allowance whose fit for a journal is [T]. See `esv-msg-rights-reference.md`. Drafts use **verse references and placeholders only**. The WEB drafts stay drafts.
- **KDP public-domain policy** ([G200743940](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200743940)) [V]: public-domain works must be differentiated by original translation, original annotations, or 10+ original illustrations, and the title must carry "(Annotated)" etc. The page doesn't say how a book that's **mostly original prompts with quoted public-domain verses** is treated. **[T], and it decides whether format 3 works on KDP.** [A] Formats 2 and 3 are mostly original structure and prompts, not a reprint, but that's my reading, not KDP's.
- **AI disclosure:** AI-generated prompts or reflections must be disclosed to KDP; AI-assisted text need not be ([G200672390](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390)) [V, earlier session]. Etsy requires AI creations to be "based on a seller's original prompts" and disclosed [V, earlier session].
- **Low-content status:** [A] format 2 is probably low-content (no ISBN needed). Formats 1 and 3 have substantive written text, so they probably aren't low-content and would need an ISBN (KDP offers a free one for non-low-content books [T recheck]).
- **Page count and print cost:** 365 one-page days runs about 370–400 pp, against 100–110 for the logs. The KDP print cost at that length needs a recheck at [G201834340](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340) [T].

## Design notes for a draft [A]
- **Undated** ("Day 1…365") lets a buyer start any day. Dated pages go stale after the year. This is a design judgment; no buyer evidence was gathered.
- **Format 2 at 6×9** fits the existing generator: same geometry QA, one repeating page. It's the closest to built-and-tested and has the least ongoing content risk. It's also the most commoditized, with a $9.98 indie comparable [V].
- **Format 3** is the most plausible differentiator, through original prompts tied to each verse. But the 365 prompts are the product. Under the hands-off rule, someone has to own their quality and voice, and AI-drafted prompts trigger disclosure. That's a decision for Cash, not something to settle here.
- **Format 1** is the heaviest writing load and competes with established publishers using licensed translations. [A] It's a poor fit for hands-off.

## Creative brief applied (Cash, via Instinct, 6694896337)
The brief: devotionals that invite Jesus, God and the Holy Spirit into **real situations** and spend time with them (prayer, listening, bringing the day's actual situation), **not generic self-help prompts**.

[A] What this changes:
- **Format 2 (plain SOAP)** doesn't meet the brief by itself. It's a study method, and its Application step can drift into self-improvement. It's useful only as a base.
- **Format 3 (hybrid)** is the one that fits, restructured around an encounter rather than a lesson. Proposed daily page, with headings for review and placeholder wording:
  1. **Today's scripture:** reference only, e.g. "[ESV ref]", until rights are cleared.
  2. **What's actually happening today:** lined space to name the real situation (work, family, money, health, a conversation coming up).
  3. **Invite:** one short line inviting Jesus, the Father or the Holy Spirit into *that* situation, written in the reader's own words. The page gives a starter, not a script.
  4. **Be still and listen:** a short unlined space for quiet, with the cue "What do you sense? What does this verse say about who He is in this?"
  5. **Respond:** prayer, and one step taken *with* Him today.
  6. **Look back** (weekly page): "Where did I see Him show up this week?"
- The prompts point the reader to God, not to self-improvement goals. Each day's reference needs a short invitation starter tied to that verse. **Those starters are the creative core.** If AI generates them, KDP and Etsy disclosure rules apply. Who writes them is Cash's decision. One option to weigh is having a person of faith write or review them; it's an editorial suggestion, not a requirement, and it trades off against cost and hands-off fit.
- [A] The same page works as a 6×9 paperback (generator-ready) and as a printable PDF. **Current target: a 30-day prototype per edition** (30 daily + 4 weekly pages + front matter, about 40 pp). Longer runs only if Cash asks for them.

## Men's and women's editions (Cash, via Instinct, 6694937892)
- **Same page structure in both:** scripture reference, today's real situation, invite, listen, respond, and the weekly look-back. One generator builds both.
- **Two separate prompt sets:** the situations named in the starters, the invitation wording, and the weekly reflections differ by audience. Cover and title differ too.
- [A] **Writing load:** 30 starters per edition for the prototype, so 60 in total. Verse references can be shared or differ, which is Hark's call in verse selection.
- [A] **Quality aim:** the differences should come from situations real men and women bring to prayer, not stereotypes or generic gendered self-help. One option for Cash to weigh, alongside cost and hands-off fit, is a man and a woman of faith reviewing their edition's starters. It's an editorial suggestion, not a gate.
- [A] **Rights:** for ESV and MSG permission requests, these count as **two products**. List both, with their own page counts, prices and volume estimates.
- [A] **Build order:** draft one edition's 30-day prototype, review it against the brief, then mirror it for the second.
