# Nature photography for scripture wall art: licence check (2026-11-06)

Cash wants some designs built on real nature photos (mountains, water, rivers). The question here is which sources allow selling **prints and POD products** with the photo on them. Research only; nothing has been bought.

| Source | Prints or POD for sale? | Owning source |
|---|---|---|
| **Unsplash** (free) | **No.** "we do not offer a license that allows selling prints or products with images printed on them". Its only route is to ask the photographer directly. | [V] https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2612317-can-i-use-unsplash-images-as-part-of-a-product-to-sell |
| **Pexels** (free) | **Only if modified.** "Don't sell unaltered copies of a photo or video, e.g. as a poster, print or on a physical product". Modifying is allowed. **Whether adding a verse counts as enough modification is not defined; that's a legal judgment [T].** Photos also can't be used as a trademark or business name. | [V] https://www.pexels.com/license/ |
| **Adobe Stock** (paid) | The **Extended licence** gives "unlimited impressions/print runs" and "the ability to create derivative products for resale, such as coffee mugs, t-shirts". Editorial assets can never be used on merchandise. The Standard licence's merchandise terms are on the full licence page, not yet read [T]. | [V] https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/web/common-questions/usage-licensing.html |
| Pinterest images | Not a licence source. Inspiration only (Instinct, 6695301074). | n/a |

## Reading [A]
- **Unsplash is out** for wall art and POD, including the "it's free for commercial use" route. Its commercial grant stops at products with the image printed on them.
- **Pexels is a grey zone.** Building a product's value on a free photo plus a verse overlay rests on an undefined "modified" test, and a dispute would land on Cash as the account holder. I'd treat it as not safe without a lawyer's view or the photographer's written permission.
- **The safe routes**, each needing Cash's decision:
  1. An Adobe Stock Extended licence per photo. That's a **spend**, so it needs Cash's approval. The price per image is [T] and hasn't been looked up as a purchase.
  2. Written permission from an individual photographer.
  3. Photos Cash or a team member takes themselves.
  4. Public-domain sources, such as some US federal agency images. Status varies per image, so it's [T] and each one would need checking.
- Resolution: whichever source is chosen, the file has to meet the POD print size at 300 DPI (Hark's POD work). Free-site downloads vary.
