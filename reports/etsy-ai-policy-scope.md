# Etsy AI rules for spreadsheet templates: what the owning sources actually say (Nov 6, about 11:55Z)

Read at source by Claude. Labels: [V] quoted from Etsy's own page, [A] interpretation.

## Creativity Standards ([etsy.com/legal/creativity](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity/), "Last updated on Jun 10, 2025")
- Digital downloads fall under **"Designed by a seller"**: "Original content created by the seller, sold as a digital download." [V]
- AI creations fit there too, but must be **"based on a seller's original prompts"**, and sellers must "disclose within their listing description if an item is created with the use of AI." [V]
- Not eligible: AI prompt bundles; bundles or PDFs of someone else's work. [V]
- Templates and spreadsheets aren't named. [V, absent]

## AI update ([Seller Handbook 1522733284810](https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1522733284810), Oct 22, 2026; no separate effective date)
- "Bot-generated listings without any human involvement aren't allowed." [V]
- "AI-generated functional guides are no longer allowed on Etsy." "The information buyers rely on to create or use your item must be your own work, not generated by AI." You'll "need to show an actual result made using your instructions, not an AI-generated or simulated result." [V]
- "AI should support your creativity and craftsmanship, not replace them." Sellers "cannot misrepresent AI-generated work as handmade or entirely their own creation." [V]
- "Different requirements apply to certain items, including ... digital ... items." The article doesn't spell those out. [V]

## What this means for our six drafts [A]
1. **"The seller's original prompts."** The prompts behind these drafts were written by Claude and the team, not by Cash. Under a plain reading they aren't the seller's original prompts, so the drafts as built don't meet "Designed by a seller" with Cash as the seller. **That's a gap, not a technicality.**
2. **Instructions buyers rely on to use the item.** The how-to-use text is squarely the kind of "information buyers rely on ... to use your item". It must be the seller's own work. That's why the Instructions sheets were left blank, but blank isn't compliance: someone still has to write them, and it has to be the seller.
3. **"Show an actual result."** For an instructions-led item, a real filled-in example (for instance Cash's own pottery numbers) fits the rule. A simulated example doesn't.
4. Whether a spreadsheet's *formulas* count as a "functional guide" isn't stated. Unresolved.

## The smallest human role these texts seem to require [A, not a legal opinion]
- Cash writes or substantially directs the design brief for each template. That would make it his original prompt.
- Cash writes the Instructions sheet in his own words.
- Cash fills in one real example from his own studio, used as the listing's actual result.
- The listing discloses AI use accurately.

That's a **one-time, per-product** contribution, not recurring. Whether it's small enough for the hands-off brief is Cash's call, and Instinct's "don't sell him another job" rule applies. It also only fits niches where Cash has real data (pottery). Chicken, landlord and glaze-recipe templates would need a seller with that real experience.
