## 1. The straight answer

I checked 16 avenues outside Etsy. The strongest evidence that buyers already pay sits in things that use what Cash already owns: pottery skill, a kiln, a production setup and a small audience. Pure "AI runs it, nobody works" businesses either failed on competition (Fiverr spreadsheet services, studio software, stock footage) or rest on operator self-reports with a sales job attached (rank and rent).

That puts two of Cash's rules in tension. **Pottery levers have the best paid-demand evidence but need production labor. Hands-off digital levers have the weakest evidence.** The way through, as I see it [A], is for AI to carry marketing, sales admin, booking, payments and bookkeeping around a pottery revenue line, while production is Cash's or a paid helper's later. That is a decision only Cash can make (see Section 6).

It is early November. Census data shows hobby, toy and game stores took about 31% of their 2016 annual sales in November and December, against about 18% for all retail ([Census](https://cdn.www.census.gov/newsroom/blogs/random-samplings/2017/11/holiday-shopping.html)) [V]. That's a proxy category, not pottery, but it suggests the next eight weeks are the best selling window of the year for a maker [A].

## 3. Qualified candidates

### Rank 1: Holiday pre-orders, then a mug club

**Product:** a limited holiday run sold as pre-orders to Cash's existing followers, then a prepaid subscription of four seasonal mugs a year.

**Paid demand:**
- A potter's mug club, sold as one product on a Squarespace site, has been "perpetually sold out with a cap of 50 members" for over a year. Members prepay a year for four mugs released in March, June, September and December ([Pottery Making Illustrated](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/in-the-studio-side-hustles-sales-mug-club)) [S, operator account in a trade magazine]. Revenue isn't disclosed.
- A live listing sells a 4-mugs-a-year pottery subscription at $140, prepaid, with shipping charged once up front ([Artisans Co-op](https://artisans.coop/products/pottery-subscription-mugs-4-per-year)) [V]. That price is a public comparable, not a sales figure.
- The holiday seasonality above [V, proxy category].

**Competition:** every potter can do this, but a mug club sells to the potter's own followers, so the competition is for their attention, not search rank [A].

**Costs:** clay, glaze, firing and shipping per mug. Platform fees depend on where it's sold. No subscription tools are needed at 50 members, since a spreadsheet tracked them in the documented case [S].

**First customers:** Cash's ~1,300 TikTok followers and any existing pottery buyers. Purchase conversion from that audience is untested (Instinct's framing) [A].

**AI vs Cash:**
| Work | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Offer design, pricing comparables, product page copy, launch posts, member spreadsheet, renewal and pickup emails, bookkeeping | AI drafts; Cash approves anything public |
| Making the mugs | Cash (or a paid helper later) |
| Pressing publish, posting from his account, payment account | Cash |

**Failure risks:** production can't keep up; breakage in shipping; followers watch but don't buy; prepaid obligations for a full year if Cash's capacity drops.

**Cheap test [T, Needs Cash]:** a 10-slot "founding members" offer posted once to existing followers. Pass if most slots sell within 14 days [A threshold]. Costs nothing before sales. Cash commits to making 40 mugs over a year if all 10 sell.

### Rank 2: Contract kiln firing for local hobby potters

**Product:** firing other people's greenware and glazed work, priced by volume.

**Paid demand [V]:** studios publish paid firing services, which shows buyers pay.
- Berkeley Potters Studio charges non-members $35.00 per cubic foot for bisque, $43.50 for cone 6 and $63.00 for cone 10 with studio glazes, with a $50 minimum ([price page](https://berkeleypottersstudio.squarespace.com/firing-services)). It has paused contract firing until January 2027.
- A Ceramic Arts Network studio example charges $0.06 per cubic inch for bisque plus glaze, so a 4 × 3 × 5 inch mug costs $3.60 ([CAN](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/a-super-handy-way-to-calculate-firing-fees)).
- Home potters without kilns actively look for firing services; one pottery site runs a guide to finding them ([The Pottery Wheel](https://thepotterywheel.com/10-easy-ways-to-find-a-pottery-firing-service-near-me/)) [V that the guide exists; demand size not measured].

**Competition:** local only. Community studios and clay suppliers are the competitors, and the nearest big one pausing (as Berkeley has) is an opening [A].

**Costs:** electricity and element wear per firing, plus shelf damage risk. No upfront spend [A]. Insurance status is unknown.

**First customers:** local pottery Facebook groups, clay suppliers' bulletin boards, and community-ed class students who lose kiln access when a class ends [A]. Any posting is outreach and needs approval.

**AI vs Cash:** AI builds the price sheet and a fee calculator, an intake form with clay-body and cone rules, booking, invoices and bookkeeping. Cash (or a helper) loads, fires and unloads, and enforces the clay rules.

**Failure risks:** unknown clay bodies melting onto shelves or elements; glaze drips; liability if a customer's work breaks; local zoning or insurance; low local demand.

**Cheap test [T]:** free first step that AI can do: map existing firing services and their prices within driving range (Needs Cash: the area, kept out of this public repo). Then, with approval, one post in one local group offering a single test firing.

**First customers:** local coffee shops and breweries that sell merch [A]. This requires outreach from Cash's identity.

**AI vs Cash:** AI builds the target list, a quote calculator, mockups, outreach drafts and follow-ups. Cash approves and sends outreach, takes any call, and makes the mugs.

**Failure risks:** custom work and revisions eat time; underquoting; deadlines around the holidays.

**Cheap test [T, Needs Cash]:** AI drafts a one-page offer and a list of 15 local businesses; Cash sends to 5. Pass if one asks for a quote.

### Rank 4: Faire wholesale

**What it is:** a wholesale marketplace where independent retailers order from makers.

**Fees [V]:** 15% commission on marketplace orders, plus a one-time $10 fee per new retailer; 0% on retailers the brand brings in through Faire Direct; $0.30 processing per order ([Faire support](https://www.faire.com/support/articles/360015893392)). Faire has a pottery category ([Faire](https://www.faire.com/discover/pottery)).

**Fit:** retailers reorder, which suits a hands-off goal better than one-off retail [A]. But wholesale pricing is roughly half of retail and it needs dependable production [A].

**Needs Cash:** whether production can support wholesale volume, and approval to apply as a brand.
